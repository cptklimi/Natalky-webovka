The part most agencies leave off their website

Two changes to Queensland tenancy law quietly moved risk onto you.

Most owners I speak to haven't had either one explained to them by their current agency. Both affect what your property earns and what it exposes you to.

Rent increases are limited to once every 12 months — and the limit follows the property, not the tenancy.

Change tenants and the clock doesn't reset. If a previous manager raised the rent at the wrong point in the cycle, you can be locked out of an increase you were counting on. The first thing I check on any new property is the date of the last increase.

Minimum housing standards apply to general tenancies.

Weatherproofing, secure locks, functioning cooking facilities, adequate ventilation. Not aspirational — required. Non-compliance is the owner's problem, and it tends to surface at the worst possible moment: a repair order, or an application to the tribunal.

Questions owners actually ask

Can I switch property managers mid-lease?

Yes. The tenancy agreement is between you and your tenant; the managing agent is appointed by you and can be changed at any time. Check your current agreement for the notice period — most are 30 or 90 days. Send it to me and I'll tell you exactly where you stand before you commit to anything.

Will my tenant be affected?

No. The lease, the rent, the bond and the terms all stay exactly as they are. Your tenant gets one letter with new contact details and payment instructions. In practice most are relieved — they usually couldn't reach the old agency either.
